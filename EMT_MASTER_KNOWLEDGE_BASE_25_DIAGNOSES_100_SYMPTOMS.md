## SECTION 2: THE 25 CORE NREMT MEDICAL PATHOLOGIES

### 1. Acute Left-Sided Congestive Heart Failure (Acute Pulmonary Edema)
- **CAD**: 72M, severe dyspnea, orthopnea, waking up gasping.
- **Pathophysiology**: Failing left ventricle backs up hydrostatic pressure into pulmonary veins, transudating fluid into alveoli.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Pink frothy sputum, bilateral crackles/rales in bases, jugular venous distention (JVD), pedal edema, hypertension (BP > 180/100).
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 210/114, HR 122, RR 32 (labored), SpO2 84% on RA.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Mistaking audible expiratory wheezes ("cardiac asthma") for bronchospasm and giving an Albuterol nebulizer. Beta-2 stimulation causes tachycardia, skyrocketing myocardial oxygen demand.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: High-flow O2 via CPAP (5–10 cmH2O), seated upright with legs dangling, Nitroglycerin 0.4mg SL (if SBP > 100 and no PDE5 inhibitors in 48h), rapid ALS rendezvous.

### 2. Acute Pulmonary Embolism (PE)
- **CAD**: 28F, sudden onset pleuritic chest pain and shortness of breath after cross-country flight.
- **Pathophysiology**: Thrombus (typically from DVT) occludes pulmonary arterial bed, creating severe V/Q mismatch and dead space ventilation.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Sudden dyspnea, pinpoint pleuritic chest pain, clear lung sounds despite profound hypoxia, calf pain/unilateral leg swelling, recent immobility or oral contraceptive use.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 100/68, HR 134, RR 30, SpO2 87% (refractory to standard O2).
- **The Lethal Trap**: Assuming clear lung sounds means respiratory system is normal, or anchoring on "hyperventilation/anxiety attack" in a young patient.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: High-flow O2 (15L NRB), calm reassurance, position of comfort, rapid transport, ALS intercept.

### 3. Ruptured Ectopic Pregnancy
- **CAD**: 24F, sudden severe lower abdominal pain and syncope in bathroom.
- **Pathophysiology**: Blastocyst implants in fallopian tube; gestational growth ruptures maternal vasculature causing hemoperitoneum and hypovolemic shock.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Unilateral sharp lower quadrant pain, missed menstrual period (6-8 weeks LMP), dizziness/syncope, referred right shoulder pain (Kehr's sign from diaphragmatic irritation), pale cool diaphoretic skin.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 82/48, HR 138 (thready), RR 26, SpO2 96%, BGL 98.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Treating syncope as vasovagal or dehydration; failing to consider any female of childbearing age with abdominal/pelvic pain as hemorrhagic shock until proven otherwise.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Supine/position of shock, maintain body warmth, high-flow O2, immediate Code 3 transport, large-bore IV access via ALS rendezvous.

### 4. Severe Anaphylaxis with Distributive Shock
- **CAD**: 19M at restaurant with acute respiratory distress and facial angioedema.
- **Pathophysiology**: IgE-mediated systemic mast cell degranulation releases massive histamine, causing profound systemic vasodilation, capillary leakage, and bronchospasm.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Inspiratory stridor (laryngeal edema), bilateral wheezing, diffuse urticaria (hives), lip/tongue swelling, hypotension.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 84/46, HR 140, RR 32 (stridor), SpO2 86% on RA.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Delaying Epinephrine IM to give Albuterol or oral antihistamines first. Only Epinephrine alpha-1 vasoconstriction reverses airway collapse.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Immediate Epinephrine 0.3mg (1:1,000) IM into anterolateral thigh, high-flow O2 via NRB or BVM, albuterol neb for refractory bronchospasm, ALS intercept.

### 5. Severe Hypoglycemia (Stroke Mimic)
- **CAD**: 56M slumped over steering wheel, bystanders report slurred speech and stroke.
- **Pathophysiology**: Serum glucose < 60 mg/dL deprives cortical neurons of essential substrate, causing focal neuro deficits and coma (neuroglycopenia).
- **Hallmark Findings**: Profuse cold diaphoresis, tachycardia, combativeness or unresponsiveness, unilateral hemiparesis or facial droop mimicking acute CVA.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 136/82, HR 118, RR 18, SpO2 98%, BGL 32 mg/dL.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Calling a "Code Stroke" and driving past appropriate treatment without checking a fingerstick Blood Glucose Level (BGL).
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Check BGL immediately. If conscious/swallowing: Oral glucose 15g buccal. If altered/unconscious: Partner/ALS D10 100mL IV or Glucagon 1mg IM.

### 6. Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH) & Cushing's Triad
- **CAD**: 61M, sudden "worst headache of life", vomiting, photophobia.
- **Pathophysiology**: Rupture of cerebral berry aneurysm into subarachnoid space; skyrocketing intracranial pressure (ICP) compresses brainstem.
- **Hallmark Findings**: "Thunderclap" headache, nuchal rigidity (stiff neck), photophobia, progressing to Cushing's Triad: Hypertension with widened pulse pressure, bradycardia, and irregular/Cheyne-Stokes respirations.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 212/118, HR 48, RR 12 (irregular), Pupils sluggish/unequal.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Giving high-dose Aspirin or Nitro under the assumption of ACS, accelerating catastrophic intracranial bleeding.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Elevate head of bed 30 degrees, maintain neutral cervical alignment, avoid hyperventilation (maintain normal ETCO2 35-45 mmHg), rapid transport to Comprehensive Stroke Center.

### 7. Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)
- **CAD**: 21F college student, deep rapid breathing, weakness, abdominal cramps.
- **Pathophysiology**: Absolute insulin deficiency forces cellular lipolysis, producing acidic ketone bodies and severe metabolic ketoacidosis with osmotic diuresis.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Kussmaul respirations (deep, rapid sighing breathing), fruity/acetone breath odor, polyuria, polydipsia, dehydration, warm dry skin.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 98/62, HR 124, RR 34 (deep sighing), SpO2 99%, BGL 540 mg/dL.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Misdiagnosing tachypnea as hyperventilation syndrome or asthma and withholding aggressive IV fluid rehydration.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: High-flow O2, monitor cardiac rhythm (risk of hyperkalemia), aggressive isotonic crystalloid fluid resuscitation via ALS, transport.

### 8. Septic Shock (Urosepsis / Pneumonia Sepsis)
- **CAD**: 84F in care home, altered mental status, caregiver states "dementia bad today."
- **Pathophysiology**: Dysregulated host systemic inflammatory response to infection causes endothelial injury, profound vasodilation, microvascular permeability, and cellular dysoxia.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Hypothermia (<96.8°F) or hyperthermia, tachypnea, tachycardia, MAP < 65, warm flushed extremities initially turning mottled, cloudy foul urine or productive cough.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 84/50 (MAP 61), HR 126, RR 26, SpO2 92%, Temp 95.8°F, BGL 110.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Dismissing altered mental status as "baseline Alzheimer's" and missing hypothermia as the geriatric fever equivalent.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: High-flow O2, keep warm, ALS fluid challenge (30 mL/kg bolus), rapid transport with hospital Sepsis Alert notification.

### 9. Acute Inferior Wall STEMI (Right Coronary Artery Occlusion)
- **CAD**: 66M, severe epigastric indigestion, nausea, sweating after meal.
- **Pathophysiology**: RCA occlusion causes infarction of diaphragmatic wall of left ventricle and right ventricle, impairing RV stroke volume and AV nodal conduction.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Epigastric ache/burning mimicking heartburn, diaphoresis, bradycardia (AV nodal ischemia), hypotension when venous return is diminished.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 106/66, HR 50 (sinus bradycardia), RR 20, SpO2 95%, BGL 118.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Administering Nitroglycerin. RV infarction makes cardiac output entirely preload-dependent; Nitro eliminates venous return, causing refractory circulatory collapse!
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: 324mg chewable Aspirin, acquire 12-lead ECG (V4R check), withhold Nitroglycerin, O2 if SpO2 < 94%, urgent transport to PCI Cath Lab.

### 10. Spontaneous Pneumothorax
- **CAD**: 19M, tall, thin, sudden sharp unilateral chest pain while exercising.
- **Pathophysiology**: Rupture of apical subpleural bleb causes air to escape pulmonary parenchyma into pleural cavity, partially collapsing lung.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Sudden unilateral pleuritic chest pain, unilaterally decreased or absent breath sounds, hyperresonance to percussion, tachypnea.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 126/78, HR 112, RR 28 (shallow), SpO2 90% on RA.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Applying positive pressure Bag-Valve-Mask (BVM) ventilation, forcing high-pressure air through the pleural tear and transforming a simple pneumothorax into a lethal Tension Pneumothorax.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: High-flow O2 via Non-Rebreather (passive), seated position, continuous monitoring for tension physiology (tracheal shift, hypotension), rapid transport.

### 11. Tension Pneumothorax
- **CAD**: 32M, progressive severe air hunger, cyanosis, distended neck veins after chest trauma.
- **Pathophysiology**: One-way valve air leak into pleural space builds suprathoracic pressure, collapsing vena cava, halting venous return and causing obstructive shock.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Severe respiratory distress, absent breath sounds on affected side, tracheal deviation away from affected side, JVD, profound hypotension, subcutaneous emphysema.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 74/42, HR 144, RR 36, SpO2 78%.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Treating as asthma or cardiac arrest without performing immediate pleural decompression.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Immediate needle thoracostomy (ALS) at 2nd intercostal space midclavicular or 5th intercostal anterior axillary line, high-flow O2, emergent Code 3 transport.

### 12. Cardiac Tamponade
- **CAD**: 45M, blunt chest trauma, distant heart sounds, severe low BP.
- **Pathophysiology**: Blood/fluid accumulation within pericardial sac compresses cardiac chambers, preventing ventricular filling in diastole.
- **Hallmark Findings**: **Beck's Triad** (Hypotension, Distended Jugular Veins, Muffled Heart Sounds) + Pulsus Paradoxus (SBP drop > 10 mmHg on inspiration), clear lung sounds.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 80/68 (narrowed pulse pressure), HR 130, RR 26, SpO2 93%.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Confusing with tension pneumothorax or CHF; listening to lung sounds reveals lungs are completely clear.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: High-flow O2, rapid ALS intercept for pericardiocentesis, rapid trauma transport.

### 13. Acute Thoracic Aortic Dissection
- **CAD**: 62M, sudden onset tearing, ripping pain between shoulder blades.
- **Pathophysiology**: Intimal tear in aorta allows blood to dissect medial layer, potentially shearing coronary arteries or carotid arteries.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Sudden maximum-intensity "ripping/tearing" pain radiating to interscapular region, unequal blood pressure between arms (>20 mmHg difference), absent radial pulses.
- **Vitals Signature**: Left Arm BP 184/102, Right Arm BP 122/74, HR 96, SpO2 97%.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Giving Aspirin or blood thinners assuming typical acute myocardial infarction, which causes fatal exsanguination.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Compare bilateral bilateral BPs, withhold Aspirin/anticoagulants, gentle handling, immediate cardiothoracic surgical center transport.

### 14. Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA) Rupture
- **CAD**: 71M, sudden tearing lower back pain, syncope, pulsatile mass.
- **Pathophysiology**: Weakening of aortic wall dilates artery until rupture occurs into retroperitoneal or peritoneal space.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Severe tearing lumbar back pain radiating to groin, pulsating midline abdominal mass, loss of distal pedal pulses, profound hemorrhagic shock.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 76/44, HR 132 (weak/thready), RR 26, skin cool/mottled.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Vigorous, deep palpation of the abdomen that ruptures a contained retroperitoneal hematoma.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Gentle abdominal exam, do NOT palpate aggressively, treat for hypovolemic shock, keep warm, immediate surgical transport.

### 15. Acute Ischemic Stroke (Large Vessel Occlusion / LVO)
- **CAD**: 74F, sudden right-sided paralysis and inability to speak at breakfast.
- **Pathophysiology**: Thrombus or embolus occludes major cerebral artery (e.g. Middle Cerebral Artery), starving neural tissue of perfusion.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Cincinnati Stroke Scale positive (Facial droop, unilateral arm drift, slurred speech/aphasia), gaze deviation, normal BGL.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 190/100, HR 88 (atrial fibrillation), RR 16, SpO2 96%, BGL 104.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Failing to determine exact "Last Known Well" (LKW) time, disqualifying patient from IV thrombolysis (tPA/TNK) or mechanical thrombectomy.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Establish precise LKW time and family contact phone number, check BGL, elevate head 15-30 degrees, Code Stroke hospital pre-notification.

### 16. Status Epilepticus
- **CAD**: 30M, continuous generalized tonic-clonic convulsions > 5 minutes.
- **Pathophysiology**: Continuous unabated neuronal electrical firing causes cerebral hyperthermia, hypoxia, and rhabdomyolysis.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Continuous or recurrent tonic-clonic motor activity without return of consciousness between seizures, cyanosis, tongue biting, post-ictal state.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 160/94, HR 148, RR 8 (agonal during seizure), SpO2 82%, Temp 102.1°F.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Forcing tongue blades or airways into teeth during active clonic contractions, causing airway obstruction and tooth aspiration.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Protect patient from physical hazards, clear airway (suction), high-flow O2, ALS intercept for IV Midazolam/Lorazepam (benzodiazepines).

### 17. Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS)
- **CAD**: 79F Type 2 diabetic, progressive profound lethargy, severe dehydration over 5 days.
- **Pathophysiology**: Relative insulin presence prevents massive lipolysis (no significant ketoacidosis), but extreme hyperglycemia drives massive osmotic diuresis and intravascular dehydration.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Extreme dehydration (sunken eyes, tenting skin), normal or shallow respirations (NO Kussmaul breathing), profound altered mental status, BGL > 600 mg/dL.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 88/54, HR 126, RR 18, SpO2 95%, BGL 840 mg/dL.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Confusing with DKA and expecting fruity breath or Kussmaul respirations; HHS patients suffer fatal vascular thrombosis from hyperosmolarity without prompt fluids.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Protect airway, ALS fluid replacement boluses, rapid transport.

### 18. Acute Severe Asthma Exacerbation (Status Asthmaticus)
- **CAD**: 16F, severe shortness of breath, silent chest, exhausted.
- **Pathophysiology**: Chronic inflammatory airway hyperreactivity triggers acute bronchoconstriction, airway wall edema, and tenacious mucous plugging.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Tripod position, accessory muscle use, initial loud expiratory wheezing progressing to "silent chest" (no air movement), pulsus paradoxus, lethargy.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 138/88, HR 142, RR 36, SpO2 82% on RA.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Interpreting absence of wheezing as "improvement" when in fact it signifies terminal respiratory fatigue and impending arrest!
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Continuous Albuterol (2.5mg) + Ipratropium (0.5mg) nebulizers with O2 at 6-8 L/min, IM Epinephrine 0.3mg if air movement too poor to inhale nebulizer, CPAP.

### 19. Severe COPD Exacerbation
- **CAD**: 68M smoker, worsening dyspnea, barrel chest, productive yellowish sputum.
- **Pathophysiology**: Chronic bronchitis and emphysema with loss of alveolar elastic recoil; dynamic air trapping leads to intrinsic PEEP and severe respiratory muscle fatigue.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Pursed-lip breathing, barrel chest, prolonged expiratory phase, coarse rhonchi and wheezing, chronic cyanosis or pink puffing.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 152/92, HR 112, RR 26, SpO2 84% on RA (baseline 88%).
- **The Lethal Trap**: Withholding oxygen out of excessive fear of "knocking out hypoxic drive"; never withhold oxygen from a hypoxic, suffocating patient!
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Titrate O2 to target SpO2 88-92%, Duoneb (Albuterol/Atrovent) neb, CPAP with caution for air trapping.

### 20. Opioid Overdose (Hypoventilation & Toxic Coma)
- **CAD**: 27M found unresponsive in public restroom with slow, shallow breathing.
- **Pathophysiology**: Exogenous mu-opioid receptor stimulation depresses medullary respiratory centers, causing hypercapnia, hypoxia, and secondary cardiac arrest.
- **Hallmark Findings**: **Opioid Toxidrome Triad** (Pinpoint constricted pupils / miosis, Respiratory depression / apnea < 8/min, Coma / unresponsiveness), track marks, cyanosis.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 96/60, HR 54, RR 4 (shallow), SpO2 72% on RA, Pupils 1mm pinpoint.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Pushing high-dose rapid Naloxone (Narcan) IV/IN before ventilating with BVM, throwing the patient into violent projectile vomiting and acute withdrawal pulmonary edema.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Open airway with NPA, ventilate with Bag-Valve-Mask at 15L O2 first! Titrate Naloxone (0.4–2mg IN/IV) until adequate spontaneous respiratory effort returns (RR 12-16).

### 21. Organophosphate Poisoning (Cholinergic Toxidrome)
- **CAD**: 48M agricultural worker, profuse vomiting, diarrhea, twitching, drooling.
- **Pathophysiology**: Organophosphates inhibit acetylcholinesterase, producing catastrophic toxic accumulation of acetylcholine across parasympathetic receptors.
- **Hallmark Findings**: **SLUDGEM** (Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Defecation, GI cramping, Emesis, Miosis) + **Killer B's** (Bradycardia, Bronchorrhea, Bronchospasm).
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 90/58, HR 42, RR 30 (copious pulmonary secretions), Pupils 1mm.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Failing to recognize toxic dermal absorption; EMT entering the hot zone without proper PPE becomes a secondary victim!
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Decontaminate patient, protect self, aggressive airway suctioning, high-flow O2, massive doses of Atropine and Pralidoxime (2-PAM) via ALS.

### 22. Environmental Heat Stroke
- **CAD**: 18M football player collapses during August practice; hot, confused, combative.
- **Pathophysiology**: Thermoregulatory system failure causes core temperature > 104°F (40°C), triggering systemic inflammatory response and encephalopathy.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Altered mental status (confusion, delirium, seizures, ataxia), hot skin (may be dry or sweating in exertional heat stroke), tachycardia, hypotension.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 94/56, HR 152, RR 28, Temp 106.2°F (41.2°C), BGL 94.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Confusing with heat exhaustion (which has normal mental status) and failing to initiate immediate aggressive whole-body cold water immersion.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Immediate active cooling: ice water immersion or wet towels + ice packs to axillae/groin/neck, fan vigorously, do not give oral fluids, ALS transport.

### 23. Neurogenic Shock (High Spinal Cord Injury)
- **CAD**: 22M diving accident, shallow water, paralyzed from chest down.
- **Pathophysiology**: Cervical/high thoracic spine disruption severs descending sympathetic autonomic tracts, leaving unopposed vagal parasympathetic tone (loss of vasomotor tone).
- **Hallmark Findings**: **The Neurogenic Triad**: Hypotension, Bradycardia (or inappropriately normal HR), and Warm, Dry, Flushed skin below level of injury (flaccid paralysis).
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 78/44, HR 50, RR 20 (diaphragmatic breathing only), Temp 97.0°F.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Expecting tachycardia and cold clammy skin in shock; treating bradycardia as cardiac rather than distributive autonomic shock.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Spinal Motion Restriction (cervical collar), supine position, high-flow O2, keep warm (loss of thermoregulation), ALS vasopressors (Norepinephrine).

### 24. Acute Peritonitis / Ruptured Appendicitis
- **CAD**: 23M, 24 hours of periumbilical pain that migrated to right lower quadrant, now rigid and screaming.
- **Pathophysiology**: Appendiceal lumen obstruction leads to ischemia, necrosis, and perforation, spreading purulent bacteria across peritoneal membrane.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Right lower quadrant pain (McBurney's point), involuntary abdominal guarding, rigid "board-like" abdomen, rebound tenderness, high fever, knees tucked to chest.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 118/74, HR 116, RR 22, Temp 102.4°F, BGL 102.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Giving oral analgesics or food/water, which delays emergent laparotomy and increases aspiration risk.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Position of comfort (knees flexed), NPO (nothing by mouth), gentle transport, monitor for septic shock progression.

### 25. Upper Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage (Esophageal Varices)
- **CAD**: 54M with history of alcoholism, sudden massive vomiting of bright red blood.
- **Pathophysiology**: Portal hypertension from hepatic cirrhosis causes massive dilatation of collateral submucosal veins in distal esophagus; rupture causes torrential bleeding.
- **Hallmark Findings**: Massive hematemesis (bright red blood and clots), signs of liver failure (ascites, jaundice, spider angiomas), melena, profound hypovolemic shock.
- **Vitals Signature**: BP 72/40, HR 144 (thready), RR 26, SpO2 91%, Skin pale/cold/clammy.
- **The Lethal Trap**: Allowing patient to remain supine, leading to catastrophic blood aspiration and asphyxiation within seconds.
- **Correct EMT Treatment**: Immediate airway protection! Position patient on side (lateral recumbent) or sitting forward, aggressive rigid Yankauer suction, high-flow O2, Code 3 rapid transport.

## SECTION 3: THE 100 MASTER CLINICAL SIGNS & SYMPTOMS MATRIX

### 🫁 Group A: Airway & Respiratory Sounds (1–20)
1. **Inspiratory Stridor**: High-pitched raspy sound at neck → Upper airway laryngeal edema (Anaphylaxis, Croup, foreign body).
2. **Expiratory Wheezing**: High-pitched musical whistling → Lower airway bronchospasm (Asthma, Anaphylaxis, COPD).
3. **Bibasilar Inspiratory Crackles (Rales)**: Popping/bubbling at lung bases → Alveolar fluid transudation (Left CHF, Pulmonary Edema).
4. **Unilateral Crackles**: Focal localized coarse rales → Consolidation/Infiltrate (Lobar Pneumonia).
5. **Absent Breath Sounds Unilaterally**: Complete silence on one hemithorax → Pneumothorax, Tension Pneumothorax, Hemothorax.
6. **Silent Chest**: Severe dyspnea with total absence of audible wheezes or breath sounds → Terminal airway closure (Status Asthmaticus).
7. **Rhonchi**: Low-pitched snoring/rattling sounds in large airways → Mucus plugging (Chronic Bronchitis, Pneumonia).
8. **Kussmaul Respirations**: Continuous deep, rapid, sighing respirations → Respiratory compensation for metabolic ketoacidosis (DKA).
9. **Cheyne-Stokes Respirations**: Crescendo-decrescendo breathing followed by apnea → Brainstem ischemia / Increased ICP (Cushing's, SAH).
10. **Agonal Respirations**: Irregular, gasping, reflex twitches (<6/min) → Brainstem anoxia, impending or active cardiac arrest.
11. **Pursed-Lip Breathing**: Exhaling against partially closed lips → Self-generated intrinsic PEEP to keep bronchioles open (COPD/Emphysema).
12. **Tripod Posturing**: Leaning forward with hands on knees → Maximizing diaphragm descent and accessory muscle efficiency (Severe Asthma/COPD).
13. **Substernal / Intercostal Retractions**: Skin sucking inward between ribs during inhalation → Severe work of breathing / high intrathoracic negative pressure.
14. **Tracheal Tug**: Downward pull of thyroid cartilage during inspiration → Extreme ventilatory exhaustion.
15. **Pink Frothy Sputum**: Aerated, blood-tinged serous fluid in oral airway → Severe alveolar pulmonary edema (Acute decompensated CHF).
16. **Rust-Colored Sputum**: Viscous blood-streaked sputum → Streptococcus pneumoniae infection (Community-Acquired Pneumonia).
17. **Refractory Hypoxia**: SpO2 < 92% failing to rise despite 15L Non-Rebreather → Right-to-left shunt or pulmonary dead space (Pulmonary Embolism).
18. **Subcutaneous Emphysema**: "Rice-Krispies" crinkly crackling sensation on chest palpation → Air escaping parenchyma into subcutaneous tissue (Pneumothorax, Tracheal rupture).
19. **Tracheal Deviation**: Shift of trachea away from midline toward unaffected side → Contralateral high-pressure air accumulation (Tension Pneumothorax).
20. **Hyperresonance to Percussion**: Hollow, drum-like note on chest percussion → Air accumulation in pleural cavity (Pneumothorax).

### 🩸 Group B: Circulation, Perfusion & Skin (21–40)
21. **Cool, Pale, Diaphoretic Skin**: Sympathetic alpha-1 vasoconstriction with sweat gland stimulation → Cardiogenic, Hypovolemic, or Hypoglycemic crisis.
22. **Warm, Dry, Flushed Skin Below Injury**: Vasomotor sympathetic tone loss with unopposed peripheral vasodilation → Neurogenic Shock.
23. **Urticaria (Hives)**: Raised, pruritic, erythematous plaques on dermis → Histamine-mediated systemic allergic reaction (Anaphylaxis).
24. **Angioedema**: Non-pitting swelling of lips, tongue, uvula, and periorbital tissue → Mast cell degranulation / Bradykinin surge (Anaphylaxis / ACE-inhibitor reaction).
25. **Capillary Refill Time > 2 Seconds**: Delayed microvascular bed refilling → Systemic peripheral hypoperfusion / Shock.
26. **Weak, Thready Radial Pulse**: Narrow stroke volume with high peripheral resistance → Decompensated Hypovolemic or Cardiogenic Shock.
27. **Bounding Radial Pulse**: Wide pulse pressure with high stroke volume or hyperdynamic state → Early Sepsis, severe Anxiety, or severe Hyperthyroidism.
28. **Pulsus Paradoxus**: Exaggerated systolic BP drop > 10 mmHg during inspiration → Cardiac Tamponade, Severe Status Asthmaticus.
29. **Jugular Venous Distention (JVD)**: Bulging internal/external jugular veins at 45-degree head elevation → Right ventricular strain, Tamponade, Tension Pneumothorax, Right CHF.
30. **Pitting Peripheral Pedal Edema**: Bilateral pitting swelling of ankles and pretibial areas → Right ventricular failure backing up into systemic veins.
31. **Unequal Bilateral Radial Pulses**: Asymmetry between left and right radial pulses → Aortic Dissection or proximal arterial occlusion.
32. **Loss of Distal Pedal Pulses**: Absent dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial pulses with cold feet → Rupturing AAA or acute arterial thromboembolism.
33. **Widened Pulse Pressure**: Systolic BP rising while diastolic BP remains low (e.g. 210/60) → Increased Intracranial Pressure (Cushing's Triad).
34. **Narrowed Pulse Pressure**: Systolic and diastolic BP converging (e.g. 84/72) → Cardiac Tamponade, severe Hypovolemic Shock.
35. **Pulsating Abdominal Midline Mass**: Palpable rhythmic pulsation above umbilicus → Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA).
36. **Mottled / Livedo Reticularis Skin**: Violaceous, net-like purplish skin discoloration → Microvascular stasis / Terminal septic or cardiogenic shock.
37. **Jaundice (Icteric Sclera)**: Yellow pigmentation of sclera and skin → Hyperbilirubinemia from hepatic cirrhosis or biliary obstruction.
38. **Spider Angiomas**: Central red arteriole with radiating spider-like vessels on chest/face → Hepatic cirrhosis / Portal hypertension.
39. **Poor Skin Turgor (Tenting)**: Pinched skin on forehead/clavicle stays pinched → Severe dehydration / Hyperosmolar state (HHS, DKA).
40. **Flushed, Hot, Dry Skin with Encephalopathy**: Total failure of evaporative cooling mechanisms → Classic Heat Stroke.

### 🧠 Group C: Neurologic & Sensory Findings (41–60)
41. **Unilateral Facial Droop**: Asymmetry of smile and nasolabial fold smoothing → Corticobulbar pathway stroke or Bell's Palsy.
42. **Pronator Drift**: Arm pronates and drifts downward when eyes closed and palms up → Contralateral upper motor neuron lesion (Stroke).
43. **Expressive Aphasia (Broca's)**: Comprehension intact, but halting, fragmented, non-fluent speech → Left frontal lobe infarction.
44. **Receptive Aphasia (Wernicke's)**: Fluent speech with meaningless neologisms, unable to comprehend speech → Left superior temporal lobe infarction.
45. **Pinpoint Constricted Pupils (Miosis, 1mm)**: Bilateral symmetric pupillary constriction → Opioid overdose or Pontine hemorrhage or Organophosphate poisoning.
46. **Unilateral Fixed and Dilated Pupil ("Blown Pupil")**: Compression of ipsilateral cranial nerve III (Oculomotor) → Uncal transtentorial herniation.
47. **Sluggish Bilateral Pupils**: Delayed constriction to light → Diffuse cerebral hypoxia, hypothermia, or central nervous system depression.
48. **Nystagmus (Involuntary Eye Jerking)**: Horizontal or vertical oscillatory eye movement → Cerebellar stroke, vestibular neuronitis, or PCP intoxication.
49. **Nuchal Rigidity (Meningismus)**: Inability to flex neck forward onto chest due to agonizing pain → Subarachnoid Hemorrhage or Bacterial Meningitis.
50. **Photophobia**: Painful sensitivity to light → Meningeal irritation (SAH, Migraine, Meningitis).
51. **Thunderclap Headache**: Pain reaching peak 10/10 intensity in < 60 seconds → Ruptured cerebral aneurysm (Subarachnoid Hemorrhage).
52. **Combativeness with Cold Diaphoresis**: Agitated, irritable, uncooperative behavior → Neuroglycopenia (Severe Hypoglycemia).
53. **Post-Ictal Confusion**: Slowly clearing disorientation, somnolence, and lethargy following seizure → Normal post-ictal phase (Rule out ongoing status).
54. **Tongue Laceration**: Lateral border tongue bite marks and bloody saliva → Generalized tonic-clonic seizure.
55. **Flaccid Paralysis Below Clavicle**: Complete loss of motor control and reflexes → Cervical spinal cord injury (Neurogenic Shock).
56. **Priapism**: Involuntary persistent penile erection → Loss of sympathetic inhibitory control over spinal vasodilation (Spinal Cord Injury).
57. **Ataxia**: Loss of coordinated muscular gait, stumbling like drunk → Cerebellar stroke, Wernicke encephalopathy, or Heat Stroke.
58. **Focal Hemiparesis Resolving Instantly with IV D10**: Hemiplegia that vanishes upon glucose delivery → Proof of Hypoglycemia Stroke Mimic.
59. **Decorticate Posturing**: Abnormal flexion of arms against chest with legs extended → Severe damage to cerebral hemispheres above midbrain.
60. **Decerebrate Posturing**: Rigid extension and internal rotation of arms, legs extended, head arched back → Severe brainstem compression / Herniation.

### 🤢 Group D: Abdominal, Somatic & Pain Qualities (61–80)
61. **Kehr's Sign**: Referred severe pain at the tip of the left or right shoulder → Phrenic nerve irritation from free blood under the diaphragm (Ruptured Ectopic, Ruptured Spleen).
62. **McBurney's Point Tenderness**: Sharp, focal tenderness 1.5–2 inches from ASIS on a line to the umbilicus → Acute Appendicitis.
63. **Involuntary Guarding**: Reflex spasm of abdominal wall muscles during light palpation → Peritoneal inflammation / Peritonitis.
64. **Board-Like Abdominal Rigidity**: Rock-hard abdominal wall resisting all palpation → Catastrophic diffuse peritonitis (Perforated viscus, ruptured appendix).
65. **Rebound Tenderness**: Pain is worse upon sudden release of hand pressure than during pressing → Parietal peritoneal inflammation.
66. **Tearing / Ripping Interscapular Pain**: Pain described as tearing between shoulder blades radiating down spine → Thoracic Aortic Dissection.
67. **Substernal Crushing "Elephant on Chest"**: Retrosternal pressure radiating to jaw, left shoulder, or arm → Acute Coronary Syndrome (STEMI / NSTEMI).
68. **Epigastric "Indigestion" Unrelieved by Antacids**: Burning ache in pit of stomach with diaphoresis and nausea → Inferior Wall STEMI.
69. **Pleuritic Chest Pain**: Sharp, knife-like pain worsened by deep inspiration and coughing → Pulmonary Embolism, Pleurisy, Pneumothorax.
70. **Hematemesis (Coffee-Grounds)**: Dark brown granular vomitus of partially digested blood → Upper GI bleeding with gastric acid interaction (Gastric ulcer).
71. **Hematemesis (Bright Red with Clots)**: Massive spurting of fresh blood from esophagus → Ruptured Esophageal Varices.
72. **Melena**: Jet black, tarry, sticky, foul-smelling stools → Degraded hemoglobin from upper GI tract hemorrhage.
73. **Hematochezia**: Bright red blood passed per rectum → Massive lower GI hemorrhage or brisk upper GI hemorrhage.
74. **Flank Ecchymosis (Grey Turner's Sign)**: Bluish-purple bruising along flank walls → Retroperitoneal hemorrhage (Ruptured AAA, Hemorrhagic Pancreatitis).
75. **Periumbilical Ecchymosis (Cullen's Sign)**: Bluish bruising around the umbilicus → Intraperitoneal hemorrhage (Ruptured Ectopic Pregnancy, Pancreatitis).
76. **Cloudy, Foul-Smelling Urine in Foley**: Turbid, purulent catheter drainage with sediment → Catheter-Associated Urinary Tract Infection leading to Urosepsis.
77. **Fruity / Sweet Acetone Breath Odor**: Volatile acetoacetate exhaled from lungs → Severe metabolic ketoacidosis (DKA).
78. **Fecal Odor on Breath**: Feculent-smelling eructation and vomiting → Advanced distal small bowel obstruction.
79. **Orthostatic Hypotension**: Systolic BP drop > 20 mmHg or pulse rise > 20 bpm upon standing → Volume depletion / Occult internal bleeding.
80. **Extreme Lower Back / Flank Pain Radiating to Groin**: Severe colicky pain in flank radiating to labia or testicle → Nephrolithiasis (Kidney Stone) or leaking AAA.

### 🧪 Group E: Vital Signs, Secretions & Toxidrome Clues (81–100)
81. **Hypothermia in Sepsis (Temp < 96.8°F / 36°C)**: Failure of hypothalamic thermoregulation in elderly → Septic Shock (Poor prognostic indicator).
82. **Hyperpyrexia (Temp > 104°F / 40°C)**: Extreme core temperature elevation → Heat Stroke or Malignant Hyperthermia.
83. **Profound Bradycardia with Hypotension (HR < 50, SBP < 90)**: Loss of sympathetic cardiac accelerators with systemic vasodilation → Neurogenic Shock or Beta-Blocker toxicity.
84. **Profound Bradycardia with Severe Hypertension (HR < 50, SBP > 200)**: Reflex vagal response to severe intracranial hypertension → Cushing's Triad (Impending brain herniation).
85. **Extreme Tachycardia (> 150 bpm) with Narrow Complexes**: Supraventricular tachycardia or extreme compensatory shock → Cardiorespiratory compromise.
86. **Hypoglycemia (BGL < 60 mg/dL)**: Low serum glucose causing brain starvation → Insulin overdose, oral hypoglycemic toxicity, sepsis.
87. **Extreme Hyperglycemia (BGL > 600–1000 mg/dL)**: Profound hyperosmolarity with osmotic diuresis → Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS).
88. **High Glucose with Acidosis (BGL 350–600 mg/dL)**: Ketoacidosis with Kussmaul respirations → Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA).
89. **Profuse Salivation & Drooling**: Copious oral secretions exceeding ability to swallow → Cholinergic crisis (Organophosphates) or peritonsillar abscess.
90. **Lacrimation (Excessive Tearing)**: Tears streaming down cheeks without emotional crying → Cholinergic toxicity (Organophosphates).
91. **Bronchorrhea (Copious Pulmonary Secretions)**: Massive fluid transudation into tracheobronchial tree ("Drowning on dry land") → Organophosphate poisoning.
92. **Fasciculations (Muscle Twitching)**: Fine involuntary contractions of muscle fibers visible under skin → Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor overstimulation (Organophosphates).
93. **Track Marks / Soft Tissue Abscesses**: Linear punctate scarring along antecubital and dorsal veins → Intravenous drug use / High risk for Opioid overdose, Endocarditis, Sepsis.
94. **Warm Extremities with Hypotension ("Warm Shock")**: Early peripheral vasodilation with high cardiac output → Hyperdynamic Phase of Septic Shock.
95. **Cold Extremities with Hypotension ("Cold Shock")**: Intense peripheral alpha vasoconstriction → Decompensated Hypovolemic, Cardiogenic, or Late Septic Shock.
96. **Asymmetric Blood Pressure in Arms (> 20 mmHg)**: Proximal aortic dissection flap compromising innominate or left subclavian artery → Thoracic Aortic Dissection.
97. **Unilateral Calf Edema with Erythema**: Swelling, warmth, and calf pain in one leg → Deep Vein Thrombosis (Source for Pulmonary Embolism).
98. **Bradypnea with Hypoventilation (RR < 8/min)**: Medullary respiratory drive depression → Severe Opioid or Sedative Overdose, or severe hypothermia.
99. **Tachypnea without Stridor or Wheezes (RR > 30/min, Clear Lungs)**: Respiratory compensation for lactic acidosis, metabolic acidosis, or early pulmonary embolism.
100. **Sudden Drop in ETCO2 (< 20 mmHg)**: Immediate loss of pulmonary perfusion or cardiac output → Massive Pulmonary Embolism or Cardiac Arrest.

## SECTION 4: NREMT OFFICIAL MEDICAL ASSESSMENT SCORECARD (36 POINTS + 5 BONUS)

### 1. SCENE SIZE-UP (4 Points)
- [ ] 1. PPE / BSI donned appropriately (1 pt)
- [ ] 2. Determines scene is safe (1 pt)
- [ ] 3. Determines Nature of Illness (NOI) / Chief Complaint (1 pt)
- [ ] 4. Determines number of patients & requests additional resources (ALS intercept) (1 pt)

### 2. PRIMARY ASSESSMENT (8 Points)
- [ ] 5. Verbalizes general impression (Sick vs. Not Sick, age, sex, distress) (1 pt)
- [ ] 6. Determines responsiveness / level of consciousness (AVPU / Glasgow) (1 pt)
- [ ] 7. Assesses Airway & Breathing adequacy (1 pt)
- [ ] 8. Initiates appropriate Oxygen Therapy (15L NRB, CPAP, or BVM) (1 pt)
- [ ] 9. Assesses Circulation: checks radial/carotid pulse quality (1 pt)
- [ ] 10. Assesses skin color, temperature, condition, cap refill (1 pt)
- [ ] 11. Identifies and manages immediate life-threats (1 pt)
- [ ] 12. Determines Transport Priority (Load and Go / Code 3 vs. Stay & Play) (1 pt)

### 3. HISTORY TAKING & SECONDARY ASSESSMENT (12 Points)
- [ ] 13. Investigates Chief Complaint with OPQRST (Onset, Provocation, Quality, Radiation, Severity, Time) (3 pts)
- [ ] 14. Gathers full SAMPLE history (Signs/Symptoms, Allergies, Medications, Pertinent past history, Last oral intake, Events leading) (3 pts)
- [ ] 15. Assesses baseline vital signs (BP, HR, RR, SpO2, BGL) (3 pts)
- [ ] 16. Performs focused physical exam tailored to complaint (Cardiovascular, Respiratory, Abdominal, Neuro) (3 pts)

### 4. REASSESSMENT & INTERVENTIONS (8 Points)
- [ ] 17. Administers correct medications according to 6 Rights and local protocols (Epi, Aspirin, Nitro, Oral Glucose, Albuterol) (3 pts)
- [ ] 18. Avoids lethal contraindications and toxic traps (2 pts)
- [ ] 19. Reassesses vital signs and patient condition every 5 minutes (Priority 1) or 15 minutes (1 pt)
- [ ] 20. Verbalizes complete Hand-Off Report to inbound ALS / receiving ED physician (2 pts)

### 5. DETECTIVE BONUS (+5 Points)
- [ ] 21. Correctly names the exact hidden pathology and explains the underlying pathophysiology before the proctor reveals it (+5 pts)

**CRITICAL CRITERIA FAILS (Automatic Retest Required):**
- Failure to initiate BSI / ensure scene safety.
- Failure to deliver high-flow oxygen to a hypoxic patient.
- Administering a medication with an absolute contraindication (e.g. Nitro to Inferior STEMI).
- Performing positive pressure ventilation on a spontaneous pneumothorax.
- Failure to recognize an immediate airway compromise or shock state.
