## THE 9 CADRE HIT-LIST TOPICS: MASTER BREAKDOWN

### 1. General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS) — Chapter 2 (pp. 69–71)
* **Origins:** Formulated by Dr. Hans Selye; describes the body's three-stage physiological response to acute and chronic stress.
* **Stage 1: Alarm Reaction (Immediate 'Fight-or-Flight'):**
  * The sympathetic nervous system stimulates the adrenal glands, releasing a massive surge of catecholamines (**epinephrine and norepinephrine**).
  * *Physiological Hallmarks:* Tachycardia (rapid pulse), tachypnea (rapid breathing), peripheral vasoconstriction (shunting blood to core), diaphoresis (sweating), dilated pupils, and heightened mental alertness.
* **Stage 2: Stage of Resistance:**
  * The body attempts to adapt and re-establish homeostasis while remaining in a state of elevated readiness. Cortisol secretion remains elevated.
* **Stage 3: Stage of Exhaustion:**
  * Prolonged, unrelieved exposure to stress completely depletes physical and psychological reserves.
  * *Pathological Consequences:* Chronic immune suppression, adrenal fatigue, peptic ulcers, cardiovascular disease, major depression, and professional burnout.
* **Exam Trap:** Do NOT confuse Selye's GAS (Alarm, Resistance, Exhaustion) with Elisabeth Kübler-Ross's 5 Stages of Grief (Denial, Anger, Bargaining, Depression, Acceptance).

### 2. EMS Decision-Making Process & Cognitive Biases — Chapter 9 (pp. 329–331)
* **Dual-Process Decision Theory:**
  * **Type 1 (Heuristic / Intuitive):** Fast, automatic pattern-matching based on past experience (e.g., an experienced medic immediately recognizing anaphylaxis). Highly efficient, but vulnerable to cognitive biases.
  * **Type 2 (Analytical / Deliberate):** Slow, methodical, protocol-driven reasoning (e.g., methodically working through an altered mental status differential). Slower, but protects against diagnostic tunnel vision.
* **The Fatal Cognitive Traps in EMS:**
  * **Anchoring Bias:** Fixating on the first piece of information received (e.g., a dispatch text stating *"40-year-old having an anxiety attack"*) and failing to adjust your diagnosis when physical findings point elsewhere (e.g., missing tachypnea caused by a pulmonary embolism).
  * **Confirmation Bias:** Actively searching only for clues that confirm your initial suspicion while ignoring or discounting contradictory objective findings.
  * **Overconfidence Bias:** Relying on clinical gut intuition without measuring baseline vital signs, breath sounds, or blood glucose.
  * **Deference to Authority:** A junior provider noticing an error by a senior paramedic but failing to speak up out of fear or intimidation. *(Countered by CRM PACE: Probe, Alert, Challenge, Emergency).*

### 3. Coping with Stressful Situations & CISM — Chapter 2 (pp. 32–35, 71–75)
* **The Three Stress Reactions:**
  * **Acute Stress:** Occurs during or immediately after the incident (tremor, diaphoresis, acute tunnel vision, or psychological paralysis).
  * **Delayed Stress (PTSD):** Manifests days, weeks, or months later; marked by intrusive flashbacks, nightmares, hyperarousal, emotional detachment, and avoidance of triggers.
  * **Cumulative Stress (Burnout):** The slow, insidious build-up of daily unaddressed stressors over years; manifests as chronic exhaustion, cynicism, interpersonal conflict, gastrointestinal issues, and depression.
* **Personal Resilience & Coping:**
  * Balanced nutrition (complex carbs and lean proteins for sustained energy; avoid sugary energy drinks and high caffeine that cause crashes).
  * Continuous hydration with water throughout the shift.
  * Sleep hygiene (7–9 hours, blackout curtains, eliminating screen time before daytime sleep).
* **Critical Incident Stress Management (CISM):**
  * **Defusing:** An informal, confidential peer-led discussion held **within 1 to 4 hours** post-incident; allows responders to vent emotions and receive initial coping mechanisms before going off duty.
  * **Critical Incident Stress Debriefing (CISD):** A formal, structured 7-phase meeting held **24 to 72 hours** post-event, facilitated by professional mental health clinicians and trained peer support teams.

### 4. Oxygen Flow Rates per Device & FiO2 — Chapters 10 & 12
* **Nasal Cannula:**
  * **Flow Rate:** **1 to 6 L/min**
  * **Delivered Oxygen (FiO2):** **24% to 44%** (~4% increase per L/min above room air 21%).
  * *Indications:* Mild respiratory distress, claustrophobia, or patients who cannot tolerate a face mask.
* **Nonrebreathing Mask (NRB) with Reservoir:**
  * **Flow Rate:** **10 to 15 L/min**
  * **Delivered Oxygen (FiO2):** **Up to 90% to 95%**
  * *Critical Pre-Administration Mandate:* **The reservoir bag MUST be pre-inflated** before placing the mask on the patient. If the bag collapses completely during inhalation, you must increase the flow rate.
* **Bag-Valve Mask (BVM) with Reservoir:**
  * **Flow Rate:** **15 L/min** attached to an oxygen source and reservoir.
  * **Delivered Oxygen (FiO2):** **Nearly 100%**
  * *Indications:* Apnea, respiratory arrest, or inadequate minute volume (hypoventilation / shallow breathing).
* **Venturi Mask:**
  * **Flow Rate:** Variable (4–12 L/min) using calibrated color-coded entrainment dials.
  * **Delivered Oxygen (FiO2):** Precise, titratable concentrations (**24%, 28%, 35%, 40%**).
  * *Indications:* Ideal for chronic COPD patients who require exact, low-flow oxygen titration.

### 5. Esophageal Varices — Chapter 19 (pp. 764–765)
* **Pathophysiology:**
  * **Portal Hypertension:** Chronic liver damage (most commonly caused by long-term **alcohol abuse / cirrhosis** or chronic viral hepatitis) obstructs normal blood flow through the liver.
  * Blood backs up into the portal venous circulation, engorging the fragile venous capillaries of the lower esophagus. These vessels become dilated, tortuous, and extremely thin-walled (varices).
* **Clinical Presentation:**
  * **Sudden, painless, massive bright red hematemesis (vomiting large amounts of blood).**
  * Rapid onset of hypovolemic shock (hypotension, tachycardia, pallor, diaphoresis).
* **Immediate EMT Management:**
  * **Airway is the absolute priority!**
  * Aggressive suctioning with a large-bore rigid Yankauer tip.
  * Place the patient on their side (**left lateral recumbent / recovery position**) to prevent massive pulmonary aspiration.
  * Administer high-flow oxygen, treat for shock (keep warm), and initiate rapid emergency transport.

### 6. Cholecystitis — Chapter 19 (pp. 761–763)
* **Pathophysiology:**
  * Acute inflammation of the **gallbladder**, typically caused by gallstones (**cholelithiasis**) obstructing the cystic duct, preventing bile drainage.
* **Classic Pain Radiation Pattern:**
  * Pain is centered in the **Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ)** and classically radiates / refers to the **RIGHT SHOULDER** or inferior tip of the right scapula.
* **The Fatty Meal Trigger:**
  * Pain typically intensifies or erupts **30 to 60 minutes after eating a fatty or greasy meal** (as the gallbladder contracts against the obstruction).
* **Associated Signs:**
  * Nausea, vomiting, low-grade fever, and **Murphy's sign** (sharp arrest of inspiration when the examiner palpates the RUQ during a deep breath). Most common in females aged 30–50.

### 7. Salmonella Bacterium — Chapters 2 & 19 (pp. 43, 765)
* **Pathogen Classification:**
  * A genus of **Gram-negative bacteria** causing infectious foodborne gastroenteritis.
* **Routes of Transmission:**
  * Ingestion of contaminated, undercooked foods: **poultry, raw eggs, unpasteurized dairy**, or contaminated produce.
  * Contact with **reptiles** (turtles, lizards, snakes).
  * Transmitted via the **fecal-oral route**.
* **Clinical Presentation & Timeline:**
  * Symptoms typically begin **12 to 72 hours** after ingestion.
  * *Hallmarks:* Severe abdominal cramps, watery diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, fever, and progressive hypovolemia/dehydration.
* **Infection Control Defense:**
  * Standard precautions: **Meticulous hand washing with soap, warm water, and friction for at least 20 seconds** is the single most effective barrier. Clean and disinfect all ambulance surfaces.

### 8. Drowning: Modern Definition & Pathophysiology — Chapter 33 (pp. 1222–1227)
* **Standardized National/WHO Definition:**
  * **"The process of experiencing respiratory impairment from submersion or immersion in liquid."**
  * *Critical Exam Note:* Outdated terms such as *"near-drowning"*, *"dry drowning"*, or *"wet drowning"* are **obsolete and medically inaccurate** under national standards.
* **Three Possible Outcomes:**
  1. Death
  2. Morbidity (ongoing neurological or pulmonary injury)
  3. No morbidity
* **Pathophysiological Cascade:**
  * Submersion → panic and breath-holding → involuntary **laryngospasm**.
  * As liquid enters the larynx, **surfactant is washed out and diluted**, causing extensive alveolar collapse (**atelectasis**).
  * Leads to severe non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema, massive intrapulmonary shunting, profound hypoxemia, and cardiac arrest.

### 9. Mammalian Diving Reflex — Chapter 33 (pp. 1224–1225)
* **Trigger Mechanism:**
  * Sudden immersion of the face in **cold water (< 68°F / 20°C)**. Mediated by the trigeminal and vagus nerves.
* **The Triad of Physiological Reflex Actions:**
  1. **Profound Bradycardia:** Heart rate dramatically slows.
  2. **Intense Peripheral Vasoconstriction:** Constriction of blood vessels to the extremities, skin, and abdominal organs.
  3. **Central Blood Shunting:** Preserves remaining oxygenated blood almost exclusively for the **brain and myocardium**.
* **Clinical Survival Significance:**
  * Drastically lowers cerebral metabolic rate and oxygen consumption.
  * Allows humans (especially **infants and small children**) to survive prolonged periods of cold-water submersion (often exceeding 30–45 minutes) without permanent brain damage.
* **Mandatory Field Rule:**
  * **Resuscitate ALL cold-water drowning victims regardless of time submerged!**
  * Initiate high-performance CPR, prevent further heat loss, and transport emergent. *"The patient is not dead until they are warm and dead!"*

## SEI CADRE TARGET HIT-LIST: 9 DIRECT PRACTICE QUESTIONS

**1. An EMT who responded to a fatal pediatric crash immediately exhibits tachycardia, diaphoresis, and acute pupil dilation. In Hans Selye's General Adaptation Syndrome, this physiological fight-or-flight state represents the:**
- A. Stage of Exhaustion
- B. Alarm Reaction
- C. Stage of Resistance
- D. Cumulative Stage
> **Correct Answer: `B`** — The Alarm Reaction is the immediate sympathetic catecholamine surge causing tachycardia, diaphoresis, pupil dilation, and heightened alertness.

**2. An EMT reads the dispatch note "40-year-old female having a panic attack" and fails to perform a thorough physical assessment or evaluate breath sounds, missing an acute pulmonary embolism. This failure is an example of:**
- A. Confirmation and Anchoring bias
- B. Skills-based execution failure
- C. Rules-based operational slip
- D. Dual-process analytical error
> **Correct Answer: `A`** — Anchoring bias fixates on initial dispatch information; confirmation bias selectively searches for panic symptoms while ignoring signs of an embolism.

**3. Which of the following stress management interventions is designed as an informal, confidential peer discussion held within 1 to 4 hours post-incident to allow responders to vent emotions before going home?**
- A. Critical Incident Stress Debriefing (CISD)
- B. Defusing
- C. Mandatory Psychiatric Assessment
- D. Operational Critique
> **Correct Answer: `B`** — Defusing is an informal peer session held within 1–4 hours. CISD is the formal 7-phase clinician-led debriefing held 24–72 hours post-event.

**4. Prior to placing a nonrebreathing mask on a patient in moderate respiratory distress, the EMT must ensure that:**
- A. The flow rate is set between 2 and 6 L/min
- B. The reservoir bag is fully pre-inflated with oxygen
- C. Both one-way side exhalation valves are removed
- D. The mask is secured loosely to allow room air entrainment
> **Correct Answer: `B`** — The NRB reservoir bag must be pre-inflated with oxygen at 10–15 L/min before placing on the patient; otherwise, the patient will breathe trapped carbon dioxide or suffocate.

**5. A 52-year-old male with a history of chronic alcohol abuse and liver cirrhosis presents with sudden, painless, massive bright red hematemesis. He is pale, diaphoretic, tachycardic, and hypotensive. Your immediate priority is to:**
- A. Administer 324 mg of chewed aspirin
- B. Suction the airway aggressively and position the patient to prevent aspiration
- C. Palpate the abdomen for a pulsating mass
- D. Insert a nasopharyngeal airway and give oral glucose
> **Correct Answer: `B`** — Ruptured esophageal varices produce massive upper GI hemorrhage. Immediate airway suctioning and recovery positioning are vital to prevent aspiration.

**6. A 42-year-old female presents with severe Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) abdominal pain that radiates to her right shoulder. She states the pain began 45 minutes after eating fried chicken. She is nauseated and febrile. You should suspect:**
- A. Acute appendicitis
- B. Acute cholecystitis
- C. Acute diverticulitis
- D. Perforated peptic ulcer
> **Correct Answer: `B`** — Cholecystitis (gallbladder inflammation) classically produces RUQ pain radiating to the right shoulder 30–60 minutes after a fatty/greasy meal.

**7. A patient reports severe abdominal cramps, watery diarrhea, and fever that began 24 hours after eating undercooked poultry. This infection is caused by which pathogen?**
- A. Hepatitis B virus
- B. Salmonella bacterium
- C. Clostridium perfringens spore
- D. Giardia protozoan
> **Correct Answer: `B`** — Salmonella is a foodborne bacterium commonly transmitted via undercooked poultry, raw eggs, and reptiles, with onset 12–72 hours post-ingestion.

**8. According to national and World Health Organization standards, "drowning" is defined as:**
- A. Death occurring within 24 hours of submersion in liquid
- B. The process of experiencing respiratory impairment from submersion or immersion in liquid
- C. Complete asphyxiation resulting solely from freshwater laryngospasm
- D. Neurological impairment that occurs exclusively after saltwater aspiration
> **Correct Answer: `B`** — The modern, standardized definition of drowning is the process of experiencing respiratory impairment from submersion/immersion in liquid (fatal or non-fatal).

**9. When a small child is suddenly submerged in cold water, facial immersion triggers the mammalian diving reflex. Which physiological response occurs?**
- A. Severe tachycardia and profound peripheral vasodilation
- B. Profound bradycardia and intense peripheral vasoconstriction, shunting blood to the brain and heart
- C. Massive hyperventilation and loss of pupillary reflexes
- D. Immediate pulmonary surfactant hypersecretion
> **Correct Answer: `B`** — The diving reflex causes bradycardia and peripheral vasoconstriction, shunting oxygenated blood to the heart and brain, dramatically extending cold-water survival.
