## MODULE 1: CHAPTER 12 — PRINCIPLES OF PHARMACOLOGY

### 1.1 National EMS Competencies & Scope of Practice
- **Core Competency:** Applies fundamental knowledge of medications that an EMT may assist or administer to a patient during an emergency.
- **Administration Modalities:**
  1. **Peer-Assisted Administration:** Administering medication to a crew member (e.g., DuoDote/Mark I auto-injector during an organophosphate/nerve agent attack).
  2. **Patient-Assisted Administration:** Assisting the patient with taking their own prescribed medication (e.g., Nitroglycerin, Albuterol MDI, Epinephrine auto-injector).
  3. **EMT-Administered Medications:** Direct administration of agency stock pharmaceuticals (e.g., Aspirin, Oral Glucose, Oxygen, Naloxone/Narcan).

### 1.2 Pharmacodynamics vs. Pharmacokinetics
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The process by which a medication works on the body (cellular biochemical actions, receptor binding).
- **Pharmacokinetics:** The action of the body upon the medication over time (**A**bsorption, **D**istribution, **M**etabolism, **E**limination).
- **Agonist:** A chemical or drug that binds to a specific receptor site and stimulates an intracellular response (e.g., Albuterol stimulates $\beta_2$ receptors).
- **Antagonist (Blocker):** Binds to a receptor site and blocks other chemicals or medications from binding (e.g., Naloxone blocks $\mu$-opioid receptors).
- **Action vs. Adverse Effects:**
  - **Action:** Expected therapeutic clinical benefit.
  - **Unintended Effects:** Predictable, mild, non-harmful side effects (e.g., headache from Nitroglycerin).
  - **Untoward Effects:** Harmful, potentially lethal adverse reactions (e.g., severe hypotension from Nitro).
- **Contraindications:** Conditions that make giving the medication harmful or fatal.
  - *Absolute:* NEVER give the drug (e.g., Nitroglycerin with Sildenafil/Viagra).
  - *Relative:* Caution required; benefits may outweigh risks under Medical Control.

### 1.3 Medication Nomenclature
- **Generic Name:** Clear, nonproprietary name assigned to the drug (e.g., ibuprofen, acetaminophen, epinephrine).
- **Trade / Brand Name:** Manufacturer's trademarked name (e.g., Motrin, Tylenol, EpiPen).
- **Over-the-Counter (OTC) vs. Prescription:** OTCs do not require a physician's prescription. EMTs must catalog all OTCs, vitamins, and herbal supplements (e.g., St. John's Wort alters metabolism of cardiac meds).

### 1.4 Routes of Administration & Absorption Rates
| Route | Category | Absorption Rate | Clinical EMT Applications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Intravenous (IV)** | Parenteral | Immediate (Seconds) | Direct vascular access; 100% bioavailability. Paramedic scope. |
| **Intraosseous (IO)** | Parenteral | Immediate (Seconds) | Needle into medullary canal of bone; equivalent to IV. |
| **Sublingual (SL)** | Parenteral | Rapid (1–3 min) | Dissolved under tongue; absorbed via vascular mucosa (Nitroglycerin). |
| **Intranasal (IN)** | Parenteral | Rapid (2–3 min) | Atomized via Mucosal Atomizer Device (MAD) into nasal capillary bed (Naloxone). |
| **Inhalation** | Parenteral | Rapid (2–5 min) | Aerosolized into alveoli for rapid bronchodilation (Albuterol MDI/SVN, Oxygen). |
| **Intramuscular (IM)** | Parenteral | Moderate (10–20 min) | Injected into vascular muscle bed (EpiPen vastus lateralis). |
| **Subcutaneous (SC)** | Parenteral | Slow (20–30 min) | Injected into fatty layer beneath skin (daily insulin). |
| **Per Os / Oral (PO)** | Enteral | Slow (30–60 min) | Absorbed through digestive tract; requires awake swallowing airway (Aspirin, Glucose). |
| **Per Rectum (PR)** | Enteral | Rapid / Reliable | Suppository into rectum; used when patient is seizing or vomiting. |
| **Transcutaneous** | Parenteral | Prolonged / Sustained | Transdermal patch absorbed through intact skin (Fentanyl, Nitro patch). |

### 1.5 Medication Forms & Delivery Technology
- **Tablets & Capsules:** Compressed powders or gelatin shells. Sublingual tablets must dissolve under tongue; chewable aspirin must be chewed completely to speed oral mucosa absorption.
- **Solutions vs. Suspensions:** A solution is a uniform liquid mixture that never separates (IV/IM injectables). A suspension contains insoluble particles that settle over time—**must be shaken vigorously before administration** (e.g., Activated Charcoal).
- **Metered-Dose Inhalers (MDIs) & Spacers:** Delivers calibrated aerosol puffs. Spacers hold the suspended cloud, eliminating hand-breath timing coordination and increasing lower airway deposition by 70%.
- **Small-Volume Nebulizers (SVNs):** Oxygen-driven (6–8 L/min) aerosolizer running continuously over 5–10 minutes. Preferred during moderate-to-severe respiratory distress.
- **Gels & Gases:** Gels adhere to oral mucosa (Oral Glucose). Gases (Oxygen) deliver supplemental oxygenation.

### 1.6 The 6 Rights of Medication Administration
1. **Right Patient:** Verify patient identity and prescription bottle label.
2. **Right Medication:** Verify label 3 times: upon retrieval, during prep, and prior to administration. Check clarity and expiration.
3. **Right Dose:** Confirm exact milligrams or puffs; verify daily maximum has not been exceeded.
4. **Right Route:** Confirm correct delivery route (SL vs. PO vs. IM vs. IN).
5. **Right Time:** Confirm clinical indication and dosing interval (e.g., Nitro q5m up to 3 doses).
6. **Right Documentation:** Document drug name, exact dose, route, timestamp, and pre/post vital signs.
- **Medical Control Orders:**
  - *Online:* Direct radio/phone physician order $\rightarrow$ **Mandatory closed-loop verbatim repeat-back**.
  - *Offline:* Standing orders and protocol algorithms.

## MODULE 2: CHAPTER 19 — GASTROINTESTINAL & UROLOGIC EMERGENCIES

### 2.1 Solid vs. Hollow Organs
| Category | Anatomical Organs | Primary Function | Consequence of Rupture / Injury |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Solid Organs** | **Liver, Spleen, Pancreas, Kidneys, Ovaries** | Metabolism, blood filtration, enzymatic/hormonal secretion. | **Massive internal hemorrhage $ightarrow$ Hypovolemic Shock**. |
| **Hollow Organs** | **Stomach, Gallbladder, Duodenum, Small/Large Intestines, Bladder** | Conduits for digestion, bile storage, waste collection. | **Leakage of acid/bile/feces $ightarrow$ Peritonitis & Sepsis**. |
| **Retroperitoneal** | **Pancreas, Kidneys, Adrenals, Aorta, IVC, Ureters** | Lie posterior to the peritoneal membrane lining. | Concealed hemorrhage; pain radiates to back/flank. |

### 2.2 Digestive & Urologic Tract Anatomy
- **Stomach:** Digests food with hydrochloric acid and pepsin to create chyme.
- **Liver:** Synthesizes bile for fat emulsification, filters portal blood toxins, stores glycogen.
- **Gallbladder:** Reservoir for bile; contracts after fatty meals to eject bile into duodenum.
- **Small Intestine:**
  - *Duodenum:* Mixes chyme with pancreatic enzymes (amylase, lipase, bicarbonate) and bile.
  - *Jejunum:* Performs the major work of nutrient absorption.
  - *Ileum:* Absorbs remaining bile salts and Vitamin B12.
- **Colon (Large Intestine):** Reabsorbs water and electrolytes; forms solid stool.
- **Spleen:** Solid lymphatic organ in LUQ; filters old RBCs and stores platelets. Highly vascular and prone to rupture.
- **Kidneys & Urinary Tract:** Retroperitoneal organs filtering metabolic waste; normal urine output is 1.5–2.0 L/day. Renal failure leads to uremia.

### 2.3 Pathophysiology of the Acute Abdomen
- **Peritoneum & Pain Types:**
  - **Parietal Peritoneum:** Lines abdominal wall; innervated by somatic nerves $ightarrow$ **SHARP, INTENSE, CONSTANT, LOCALIZED PAIN**.
  - **Visceral Peritoneum:** Covers organs; innervated by autonomic nerves $ightarrow$ **DULL, CRAMPY, ACHING, POORLY LOCALIZED PAIN** with nausea.
- **Peritonitis:** Inflammation of peritoneum causing paralysis of intestinal smooth muscle (**ILEUS**).
- **Ileus:** Halts peristalsis $ightarrow$ gas/fluid distension $ightarrow$ stomach empties by **emesis (vomiting)**.
- **Guarding & Rigidity:**
  - *Voluntary Guarding:* Patient tenses wall from fear/anticipation.
  - *Involuntary Rigidity:* Reflex rock-hard muscle spasm indicating advanced peritonitis.
- **Rebound Tenderness (Blumberg's Sign):** Severe pain upon sudden release of deep palpation.

### 2.4 Acute Abdomen Pathology Profiles
| Pathology | Quadrant / Location | Hallmark Clinical Presentation | Critical EMT Care |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acute Appendicitis** | **Periumbilical migrating to RLQ** (McBurney's Point) | Anorexia, fever, nausea, rebound tenderness. Sudden relief = rupture! | Strictly NPO, knees flexed, gentle transport. |
| **Acute Cholecystitis** | **Right Upper Quadrant (RUQ) & Right Shoulder** | Sharp RUQ pain referred to right scapula **30–60 min after fatty meals** (Murphy's sign). | Position of comfort, NPO, antiemetic care. |
| **Acute Pancreatitis** | **Epigastric radiating to Back** | Severe boring pain straight through to back; alcohol abuse or gallstone etiology. | Third-spacing shock management, high-flow O2. |
| **Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD)** | Epigastric | Burning pain relieved temporarily by food; melena, hematemesis, perforation risk. | Monitor for peritonitis rigidity, keep NPO. |
| **Esophageal Varices** | Upper GI / Esophagus | Cirrhosis/portal HTN; **sudden massive painless bright red vomiting**, rapid shock. | **Aggressive suctioning**, recovery position, shock care. |
| **Mallory-Weiss Tear** | Gastroesophageal Junction | Mucosal tears caused by violent retching/vomiting (alcohol, bulimia). | Airway maintenance, suction on standby. |
| **Diverticulitis** | **Left Lower Quadrant (LLQ)** | Fecal entrapment in colonic pouches; LLQ pain, fever, altered bowel habits. | NPO, supportive transport. |
| **Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA)** | Midline / Lower Back | Ballooned aorta; **tearing back pain, pulsatile mass, femoral pulse deficits**. | **Do NOT aggressively palpate mass!** Gentle handling. |
| **Kidney Stones (Renal Calculi)** | Flank radiating to Groin | **Severe colicky flank pain, hematuria, writhing restlessness (cannot sit still)**. | Non-peritoneal; position of comfort. Rule out AAA. |
| **Strangulated Hernia** | Groin / Inguinal, Umbilicus | Irreducible formerly reducible lump, severe pain, tender, purple/blue discoloration. | True surgical emergency; ischemic bowel. NPO. |

### 2.5 GI Bleeds: Upper vs. Lower
- **Upper GI Bleeds:** Proximal to ligament of Treitz (esophagus, stomach, duodenum).
  - *Hematemesis:* Bright red blood vomiting.
  - *Coffee-Ground Emesis:* Digested/oxidized blood vomiting.
  - *Melena:* Dark, tarry, foul-smelling digested blood stools.
- **Lower GI Bleeds:** Distal to duodenum (colon, rectum).
  - *Hematochezia:* Bright red blood passed per rectum.

### 2.6 Dialysis & Renal Emergencies
- **The AV Fistula / Graft Golden Rule:** **NEVER TAKE BLOOD PRESSURE, START AN IV, OR DRAW BLOOD FROM AN ARM WITH AN AV FISTULA OR SHUNT!** Compression clots the graft.
- **Shunt Hemorrhage:** Apply direct, firm, pinpoint gloved pressure over puncture site. Apply tourniquet if life-threatening hemorrhage persists.
- **Missed Dialysis Complications:** Fluid overload $ightarrow$ pulmonary edema; Hyperkalemia $ightarrow$ lethal cardiac arrest; Uremic toxicity.
- **Peritoneal Dialysis Peritonitis:** Recognized by **cloudy, turbid, yellowish dialysate effluent drainage** and abdominal pain.

### 2.7 Patient Assessment & Emergency Care
1. **Exam Sequence:** **Inspect $ightarrow$ Auscultate $ightarrow$ Palpate**.
2. **The Golden Rule:** **ALWAYS PALPATE THE MOST PAINFUL QUADRANT LAST!**
3. **Positioning:** Transport in position of comfort, **knees flexed (bent)** with pillows underneath to relax the abdominal wall.
4. **Strictly NPO:** Nothing by mouth (no water, no food, no oral meds) to protect surgical anesthesia candidacy.
5. **Shock Management:** High-flow O2, warm blankets, rapid transport.

## MODULE 3: SLIDE REVIEW QUESTIONS & DETAILED RATIONALES

1. **Pharmacology is defined as:** Study of drugs and medications (*Answer: B*). Pharmacodynamics = physiological effects; Pharmacokinetics = body's actions on drugs.
2. **Fastest route of administration:** Intravenous (IV) / Intraosseous (IO) (*Answer: B*). Enters bloodstream immediately with 100% bioavailability.
3. **Organ lying in retroperitoneal space:** Pancreas (*Answer: B*). Pancreas, kidneys, and aorta lie behind the peritoneum.
4. **Which is NOT a solid organ:** Gallbladder (*Answer: D*). Gallbladder is hollow (stores bile); liver, spleen, and kidneys are solid.
5. **Patient with PID presenting with acute severe diffuse tenderness:** Peritonitis (*Answer: A*). Diffuse pain indicates generalized peritoneal inflammation, unlike localized appendicitis/cholecystitis.
6. **Most common vital sign finding in acute abdomen:** Tachycardia (*Answer: D*). Heart rate > 100 bpm from sympathetic pain response.
