## SCENARIO 01: ANTERIOR STEMI / ACUTE CORONARY SYNDROME
**Domain:** Cardiovascular Emergencies | **Patient:** Mike Roberts, 48M | **Setting:** Commercial construction jobsite | **Complaint:** Crushing substernal chest pressure

### 1. Clinical Presentation & Dispatch Prompt
> **CAD Dispatch:** "Medic 4, respond lights and sirens to 1420 North Industrial Way, construction site, for a 48-year-old male complaining of severe acute chest pain while unloading materials. Clear and warm conditions."

**Core Clinical Clues & Diagnostic Hallmark:**
- Heavy 'crushing' substernal chest pressure radiating down left shoulder and arm (OPQRST Q & R)
- Hypertensive (160/88 mmHg) and tachycardic (102 bpm) sympathetic drive
- Pale, cool, diaphoretic skin with active nausea
- Clear bilateral lung sounds (no pulmonary edema)
- Prescribed Nitroglycerin on scene; no contraindications (no ED meds, no active bleeding)

### 5. Critical Traps & Proctor Grading Rubric
**Automatic Failure Traps (Critical Rubric Checkpoints):**
- ⚠️ **Failure to verbalize BSI precautions and scene safety prior to touching patient.**
- ⚠️ **Failure to recognize acute coronary syndrome as an immediate high-priority load-and-go emergency.**
- ⚠️ **Administering nitroglycerin without explicitly screening for phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (Viagra/Cialis within 24-48 hrs).**
- ⚠️ **Administering nitroglycerin without checking systolic BP (must be >100 mmHg).**
- ⚠️ **Failing to perform closed-loop verbatim repeat-back of Medical Control physician orders.**

| Suspected Condition | Differentiating Hallmark Clue | Treatment Impact / Why It Matters |

| **Gastroesophageal Reflux (GERD)** | Burning epigastric pain provoked by lying flat, relieved by antacids | Always treat as ACS until ruled out by serial troponins and 12-lead ECG. |

## SCENARIO 02: ACUTE INFERIOR-RV STEMI (THE NITROGLYCERIN PRELOAD TRAP)
**Domain:** Cardiovascular Emergencies | **Patient:** Arthur Miller, 62M | **Setting:** Living room recliner | **Complaint:** Severe epigastric burning, nausea, extreme weakness

### 1. Clinical Presentation & Dispatch Prompt
> **CAD Dispatch:** "Medic 4, respond priority 1 to 842 Elmwood Drive for a 62-year-old male with severe weakness, indigestion, and profound nausea. Caller sounds sluggish."

**Core Clinical Clues & Diagnostic Hallmark:**
- Epigastric burning discomfort often mistaken for heartburn/indigestion
- Severe sinus bradycardia (HR 48 bpm) from right coronary artery (RCA) ischemia feeding the AV node
- Borderline systolic blood pressure (BP 108/64 mmHg)
- Profuse cold diaphoresis and gray/ashen skin
- LETHAL TRAP: Nitroglycerin causes catastrophic venodilation, destroying preload in right ventricular infarcts and triggering profound shock/cardiac arrest

- ⚠️ **Delaying transport to search for fetal heart tones (fetal survival depends entirely on rapid hospital delivery).**
- ⚠️ **Packing the vagina with gauze or towels (causes severe infection and conceals massive blood pooling).**

## SCENARIO 25: NEONATAL RESUSCITATION OF A MECONIUM-DEPRESSED NEWBORN
**Domain:** Pediatric & Neonatal Emergencies | **Patient:** Infant Boy Miller, 0 minutes (newborn)M, Full term | **Setting:** Living room hardwood floor following precipitous delivery | **Complaint:** Born limp, apneic, cyanotic, covered in thick pea-soup meconium

### 1. Clinical Presentation & Dispatch Prompt
> **CAD Dispatch:** "Medic 4, respond priority 1 lights and sirens to 1422 Elmwood Street for a baby born at home. Father states baby is blue, completely limp, and not breathing."

**Core Clinical Clues & Diagnostic Hallmark:**
- Precipitous home delivery with thick 'pea-soup' meconium-stained amniotic fluid
- Depressed Newborn Presentation: Limp muscular tone, cyanotic trunk and extremities, APNEIC (no spontaneous cry or respirations)
- THE NEONATAL RESUSCITATION PROGRAM (NRP) GOLDEN MINUTE: 1. Warm, dry, stimulate, position head in 'sniffing position' (do NOT hyperextend!), clear airway with bulb syringe (mouth then nose); 2. Reassess: If still apneic or HR < 100 bpm -> INITIATE POSITIVE PRESSURE VENTILATION (PPV) via neonatal BVM with room air (21% O2) at 40 to 60 breaths/min for 30 seconds
- THE RULE OF 60 (CHEST COMPRESSIONS): If heart rate remains LESS THAN 60 BEATS PER MINUTE despite 30 seconds of effective PPV -> BEGIN CHEST COMPRESSIONS IMMEDIATELY at a 3:1 COMPRESSION-TO-VENTILATION RATIO (90 compressions and 30 breaths = 120 events/min) using the two-thumb encircling-hands technique, and increase O2 to 100%
- APGAR Scoring: 1-minute APGAR is 1 (Pulse 1, Respiration 0, Tone 0, Reflex 0, Color 0)

### 5. Critical Traps & Proctor Grading Rubric
**Automatic Failure Traps (Critical Rubric Checkpoints):**
- ⚠️ **Failing to initiate positive pressure ventilations when newborn is apneic or HR < 100 bpm after 30 seconds of drying/stimulating.**
- ⚠️ **Failing to initiate chest compressions when heart rate remains < 60 bpm despite 30 seconds of effective PPV (The Rule of 60).**
- ⚠️ **Suctioning the nose before the mouth ('M before N' rule; suctioning nose first triggers gasping and aspiration of pharyngeal meconium).**
- ⚠️ **Hyperextending the newborn's neck (causes immediate airway obstruction; must maintain neutral 'sniffing position').**
- ⚠️ **Allowing the newborn to become hypothermic (cold stress triggers pulmonary hypertension, acidosis, and secondary arrest).**
- ⚠️ **Using a 15:2 or 30:2 compression-ventilation ratio (neonatal CPR requires a strict 3:1 ratio: 90 compressions to 30 breaths per minute!).**
