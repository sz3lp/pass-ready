## PART II: ANSWER KEY & DETAILED CLINICAL RATIONALES

### Question 1: Correct Answer: `C. Intravenous (IV)`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Intravenous (IV) injection delivers medication immediately into the bloodstream, bypassing all absorption barriers and resulting in an immediate (100% bioavailability) onset of action. Intraosseous (IO) has an equivalent rate. Sublingual is rapid but mucosal; IM takes 10–20 minutes; SubQ has slow, prolonged absorption.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 498–500 (Routes of Administration)*

### Question 2: Correct Answer: `B. Antagonist`
- **Clinical Rationale:** An antagonist (or blocker) binds to a cellular receptor site without activating it, thereby preventing other natural chemicals or agonist drugs from binding and initiating a response. An agonist stimulates the receptor to produce an effect.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, p. 497 (How Medications Work)*

### Question 3: Correct Answer: `B. Generic name`
- **Clinical Rationale:** The generic name (e.g., acetaminophen, ibuprofen, albuterol) is the nonproprietary, universal name assigned by the US Adopted Names Council and is not protected by trademark. The trade or brand name (e.g., Tylenol, Advil, Ventolin) is capitalized and owned by the manufacturer. The chemical name describes the exact molecular structure (e.g., N-acetyl-para-aminophenol).
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 496–497 (Medication Names)*

### Question 4: Correct Answer: `A. Right patient, right medication, right dose, right route, right time, right documentation`
- **Clinical Rationale:** The Six Rights of Medication Administration are: (1) Right Patient, (2) Right Medication, (3) Right Dose, (4) Right Route, (5) Right Time, and (6) Right Documentation. In addition, the EMT must always verify medication expiration date and inspect for physical clarity/contamination.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 505–506 (The Six Rights)*

### Question 5: Correct Answer: `B. The patient took sildenafil (Viagra) 18 hours ago for erectile dysfunction`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5) inhibitors such as sildenafil (Viagra) or vardenafil (Levitra) taken within 24 hours, or tadalafil (Cialis) taken within 48 hours, cause profound, fatal, irreversible systemic hypotension when combined with nitrates. Nitroglycerin is also contraindicated if SBP < 100 mmHg or if intracranial bleeding is suspected.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 512–514; King County BLS Protocols (Skill Sheet #65)*

### Question 6: Correct Answer: `B. Suspension`
- **Clinical Rationale:** A suspension is a mixture of finely divided, insoluble drug particles suspended throughout a liquid vehicle that will separate upon standing and must be shaken prior to administration. A solution is a homogeneous mixture that never separates.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 501–503 (Medication Forms)*

### Question 7: Correct Answer: `C. Inhibit platelet aggregation and prevent the coronary thrombus from enlarging`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) is an antiplatelet agent, NOT a fibrinolytic (clot buster) or anticoagulant. It inhibits the enzyme cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1), blocking thromboxane A2 production, preventing platelets from clumping together and growing the existing coronary thrombus.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 510–511; Chapter 17, p. 668*

### Question 8: Correct Answer: `A. Alpha-1 vasoconstriction, Beta-1 positive inotropy/chronotropy, and Beta-2 bronchodilation`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Epinephrine is a sympathomimetic hormone with non-selective adrenergic activity: Alpha-1 receptor stimulation causes intense vasoconstriction (reverses vascular collapse and hypotension, reduces airway edema); Beta-1 stimulation increases heart rate and contractility; Beta-2 stimulation causes profound bronchodilation.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 516–518; Chapter 21, pp. 811–814*

### Question 9: Correct Answer: `B. 0.3 mg IM in the anterolateral aspect of the thigh (vastus lateralis)`
- **Clinical Rationale:** The adult epinephrine auto-injector delivers 0.3 mg of 1:1,000 epinephrine intramuscularly into the anterolateral mid-thigh (vastus lateralis). The pediatric auto-injector (EpiPen Jr) delivers 0.15 mg for patients under 66 lbs (30 kg).
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, p. 517; Chapter 21, p. 813*

### Question 10: Correct Answer: `B. Reverse opioid-induced respiratory depression and restore adequate spontaneous breathing`
- **Clinical Rationale:** The sole therapeutic objective of naloxone (Narcan) is to restore adequate spontaneous ventilations and oxygenation, NOT to wake the patient fully into acute agitation or severe narcotic withdrawal. Ventilations must be supported with BVM while preparing Narcan.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 518–520; Chapter 22, pp. 836–838*

### Question 11: Correct Answer: `B. The half-life of naloxone is often shorter than the half-life of the opioid, leading to recurrent respiratory arrest`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Naloxone has a duration of action of approximately 30–90 minutes, whereas many opioids (such as methadone, sustained-release oxycodone, or fentanyl analogs) remain active for several hours. As naloxone wears off, the patient can easily lapse back into respiratory arrest.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, p. 519; Chapter 22, p. 837*

### Question 12: Correct Answer: `C. Oral glucose gel placed between the cheek and gum (buccal)`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Oral glucose is indicated for conscious, symptomatic hypoglycemic patients (< 60 mg/dL) who possess an intact gag reflex and the ability to swallow. It is administered as a gel buccally (between the cheek and gums) or swallowed.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 509–510; Chapter 20, pp. 787–789*

### Question 13: Correct Answer: `B. Repeat the physician's order back word-for-word (echo technique) and confirm authorization before administering`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Whenever an EMT receives an online medical direction order, the 'echo technique' (verbatim repeat-back) is legally and clinically mandatory. This confirms that the order was heard accurately and provides an opportunity to clarify any misunderstandings before administering the drug.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 506–507 (Medical Direction)*

### Question 14: Correct Answer: `C. The spacer holds the aerosolized medication in suspension, allowing deeper inhalation into the lower airways rather than impacting the posterior pharynx`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Without a spacer, up to 80% of an MDI dose impacts the back of the pharynx and is swallowed. A spacer suspends the fine aerosol droplets, allowing the patient to inhale slowly and deeply, ensuring maximum delivery into the lower bronchioles.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 504–505 (Inhaled Medications & Spacers)*

### Question 15: Correct Answer: `C. Intravenous (IV) push fentanyl for severe trauma pain`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Intravenous push opioid narcotics like fentanyl and morphine are strictly Advanced Life Support (ALS / Paramedic) scope of practice. BLS medications include oxygen, oral glucose, aspirin, assisted nitroglycerin, assisted/administered epinephrine auto-injectors, naloxone, and assisted albuterol MDIs.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 12, pp. 508–509 (Medications Carried on the BLS Unit)*

### Question 16: Correct Answer: `B. Two umbilical arteries carry deoxygenated blood from the fetus to the placenta; one umbilical vein carries oxygenated blood from the placenta to the fetus`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Remember the mnemonic 'AVA' (Artery-Vein-Artery): Two smaller umbilical arteries carry deoxygenated, waste-laden blood from the fetal internal iliac arteries to the placenta. One larger umbilical vein carries freshly oxygenated, nutrient-rich blood from the placenta to the fetal ductus venosus/inferior vena cava.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, pp. 1243–1245 (Anatomy and Physiology of Pregnancy)*

### Question 17: Correct Answer: `C. Supine hypotensive syndrome caused by inferior vena cava compression`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Supine hypotensive syndrome occurs when the heavy gravid uterus compresses the inferior vena cava against the spine in the supine position. This drastically impairs venous return to the right atrium, causing a sudden drop in cardiac output and profound hypotension. It is immediately treated by tilting the patient or placing her in the left lateral recumbent position (or manually displacing the uterus to the left).
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, pp. 1246–1247*

### Question 18: Correct Answer: `A. Hypertension (> 140/90 mmHg), severe headache/visual disturbances, and sudden edema of the face and hands`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Preeclampsia typically develops after the 20th week of gestation and is defined by systemic hypertension (> 140/90 mmHg), proteinuria (excess protein in urine), persistent frontal headache, visual disturbances (spots, flashing lights, blurred vision), and sudden swelling/edema in the hands, face, and ankles.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, p. 1248 (Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy)*

### Question 19: Correct Answer: `B. Eclampsia is marked by the onset of generalized tonic-clonic seizures or coma resulting from severe hypertension`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Eclampsia is defined as the progression of preeclampsia to generalized seizures or coma. Seizures severely compromise maternal and fetal oxygenation. Management includes maintaining the airway, suctioning, high-flow O2, left lateral positioning, ALS intercept (for magnesium sulfate administration), and minimizing light/auditory stimuli.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, p. 1248*

### Question 20: Correct Answer: `B. Placenta previa`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Placenta previa occurs when the placenta implants abnormally low in the uterus, either partially or completely covering the internal cervical os. As the cervix dilates and effaces in the third trimester, placental vessels tear, resulting in characteristic PAINLESS, BRIGHT RED vaginal bleeding with a soft, non-tender uterus.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, pp. 1249–1250 (Bleeding Complications)*

### Question 21: Correct Answer: `B. Severe, constant tearing abdominal and back pain, dark vaginal bleeding (or concealed bleeding), and a rigid, board-like uterus`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Abruptio placentae is the premature detachment of the placenta from the uterine wall. Classic presentation is sudden, excruciating, tearing abdominal/back pain, dark venous blood (which may be partially or completely concealed behind the placenta), a rigid, tender 'board-like' uterus, and profound signs of maternal hypovolemic shock.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, pp. 1249–1250*

### Question 22: Correct Answer: `A. Ruptured ectopic pregnancy`
- **Clinical Rationale:** A ruptured ectopic pregnancy (fertilized ovum implanted outside the uterus, usually in the fallopian tube) is a life-threatening obstetrical surgical emergency. Classic findings: sharp unilateral lower quadrant pain, missed menstrual period, syncope/shock, and referred pain to the ipsilateral shoulder (Kehr's sign, caused by blood irritating the diaphragm).
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, p. 1249; Chapter 24, p. 888*

### Question 23: Correct Answer: `B. The second stage (expulsion stage)`
- **Clinical Rationale:** The three stages of labor are: (1) First stage (Dilation stage): Begins with the onset of regular uterine contractions and ends when the cervix is fully dilated to 10 cm and fully effaced (usually the longest stage); (2) Second stage (Expulsion stage): Begins with full cervical dilation and ends with delivery of the newborn (crowning occurs here); (3) Third stage (Placental stage): Begins after newborn delivery and ends with expulsion of the placenta (5–30 minutes).
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, pp. 1253–1255 (The Stages of Labor)*

### Question 24: Correct Answer: `B. Prepare immediately for field delivery; do not attempt transport`
- **Clinical Rationale:** When contractions are less than 2 minutes apart, the mother feels an uncontrollable urge to push or move her bowels, and crowning is visible on visual inspection, delivery is imminent. Delivery must be performed immediately on scene. Moving the patient or delaying delivery risks unassisted precipitous birth in an uncontrolled environment.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, pp. 1255–1257 (Decision: Deliver on Scene vs. Transport)*

### Question 25: Correct Answer: `B. Apply gentle, cupped support over the infant's cranium with a sterile gloved hand to guide delivery and prevent explosive birth`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Gentle, cupped manual support of the fetal head prevents sudden 'explosive' delivery, which could cause severe perineal lacerations in the mother and rapid intracranial pressure changes in the neonate. Never push the head back into the vagina.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, pp. 1258–1262 (Normal Delivery Procedures)*

### Question 26: Correct Answer: `A. Attempt to gently slip the cord over the infant's head; if it is too tight, clamp it twice 2 inches apart and cut between the clamps`
- **Clinical Rationale:** A nuchal cord (cord around the neck) occurs in up to 30% of deliveries. First, attempt to slip the loop gently over the baby's head. If the cord is too tight to slip, immediately apply two umbilical clamps approximately 2 inches apart and cut the cord between the clamps, then proceed rapidly with delivery.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, p. 1263 (Nuchal Cord Management)*

### Question 27: Correct Answer: `B. Insert a sterile, gloved hand into the vagina and gently push the presenting fetal part UP and OFF the umbilical cord, maintaining position until arrival at the hospital`
- **Clinical Rationale:** A prolapsed cord is an extreme obstetrical emergency because the presenting fetal part compresses the cord against the pelvis, obliterating fetal blood and oxygen supply. The EMT MUST insert a sterile gloved hand into the vagina to physically push the baby's head/body OFF the cord, maintaining this position continuously throughout emergency transport. Position the mother in knee-chest or exaggerated Trendelenburg, cover exposed cord with moist sterile dressing, and transport emergent.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, pp. 1271–1272 (Prolapsed Umbilical Cord)*

### Question 28: Correct Answer: `B. Insert a gloved hand into the vagina, forming a 'V' with the index and middle fingers on either side of the infant's nose and mouth, pushing the vaginal wall away to create an air passage`
- **Clinical Rationale:** In breech delivery with head entrapment (> 3 minutes), the infant will attempt to breathe while compressed against the vaginal wall, resulting in asphyxiation. The EMT must insert a gloved 'V-finger' into the vagina along the infant's face, pushing the vaginal mucosa away from the infant's nose and mouth to maintain an airway. Maintain this position while initiating immediate emergent transport.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, pp. 1270–1271 (Breech Delivery)*

### Question 29: Correct Answer: `A. Begin positive pressure ventilation (BVM) with room air (21% O2) at 40 to 60 breaths/min for 30 seconds`
- **Clinical Rationale:** The Neonatal Resuscitation Rule of 100: If the newborn's heart rate is < 100 beats/min OR if the infant is apneic/gasping after initial warming and stimulation, immediately initiate positive pressure ventilation (PPV via infant BVM) at 40–60 breaths/min using room air (21% O2) for 30 seconds. Chest compressions are only initiated if HR remains < 60 bpm after 30 seconds of effective ventilations.
- **Textbook & Curriculum Reference:** *AAOS 12th Ed., Chapter 34, pp. 1265–1269 (Neonatal Resuscitation Algorithm)*

### Question 30: Correct Answer: `C. 9`
- **Clinical Rationale:** Calculating APGAR: Appearance = 1 (body pink, extremities blue / acrocyanosis); Pulse = 2 (> 100 bpm, actual 124); Grimace = 2 (vigorous cry, active grimace); Activity = 2 (active movement, flexed extremities); Respiration = 2 (strong, lusty cry). Total score = 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 9. A score of 7 to 10 indicates a healthy, vigorous newborn requiring standard routine care.
